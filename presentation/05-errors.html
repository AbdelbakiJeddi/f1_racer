Two points are enoughnear z ≈ 0.45 m · far z ≈ 0.84 m

After block 4 we have the track centre on each band, in metres: xnear and xfar. The near point says where the track is. The line from near to far says where it is going.

e_latclip(x_near / half_width, −1, 1)+ = centre is right · ±1 = on a line
e_headingatan2(x_far − x_near, z_far − z_near)+ = track heads right · radians
confidence(conf_near + conf_far) / 20 … 1
Dividing by half_width (0.30 m) makes e_lat independent of track width: ±1 always means "on the boundary".

The same formulas applied to the simulator's exact centre give the "truth". The detected values track it within a fraction of a degree.

A chord, not a tangentcurvature shows up early

On a bend the chord is not the track direction at the car. It averages the direction over the look-ahead. For the synthetic track, centre x(z) = offset + tan(yaw)·z + ½·curvature·z², so:

(x_far − x_near)/(z_far − z_near) = tan(yaw) + ½·curvature·(z_near + z_far) = tan(yaw) + 0.64·curvature

e_heading = car-relative yaw plus a look-ahead term from curvature. Moving the far band farther increases how early bends show up.
