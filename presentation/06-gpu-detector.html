6 · The GPU TrackDetector

The production detector used during RL training: the same five blocks, run on a batch of N cars at once on the GPU. Source: src/leatherback_racer/vision/track_detector.py (247 lines).

Follow the tensordetect_detailed()

Thousands of cars are simulated in parallel, and a Python loop over them would be the bottleneck. Every step keeps N as the first dimension, so the whole batch moves through each line together.

Nine steps from (N, 128, 128, 4) to (N, 3), and no Python loop over cars.

torch.where(cond, a, b) computes both a and b for every car, then picks per element. A few wasted FLOPs buy zero branching on the GPU.

Where 0.525, 0.990 and 0.532 come fromenv_cfg.py camera

The GPU code measures across the image in normalised units, −1 (left edge) … +1 (right edge). A band's visible half-width in metres turns that into metres: y = x_norm · half_width_visible. That is the pinhole formula of page 3 with u − cx = 64·x_norm, so the constants are a camera model, pre-computed.

The weakness: one centre per halfsharp bends

Splitting at the image centre assumes the left line stays in the left half. On a sharp right bend the far-left line crosses u = 64. The left half goes empty, and the code concludes "only the right line is visible", using a centre of mass that averages both lines.

Pairing by expected width (lab) survives bends. Pairing by image half (GPU) is cheaper to batch but assumes a nearly straight road. Whether Isaac's bends reach this regime is measurable with scripts/test_vision_pipeline_live.py.
