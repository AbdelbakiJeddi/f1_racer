3 · Camera model

Block 3: turn image pixels into metres on the ground, so the outputs mean the same thing on every row. Source: labs/lanedet/camera.py. Synthetic camera: f = 64 px, h = 0.25 m, horizon row vh = 35.84.

A metre shrinks with distancecamera.py · width_px()

The track is 0.6 m wide everywhere. In the image it is 86 px wide at the near band and 46 px at the far band. Pixel distances only mean something once you know which row they are on.

Pixels per metre grows linearly with distance below the horizon, (v − vh)/h. That one fact gives the whole camera model.

Why: similar trianglespinhole camera, level

Every ray passes through one point, the pinhole. A ground point at distance z sends a ray that lands on image row v. The farther the point, the flatter the ray and the closer it lands to the horizon row.

forwardv − vh = f·h / z→z = f·h / (v − vh)
sidewaysu − cx = f·x / z→x = h·(u − cx) / (v − vh)
In the sideways formula f cancels. Lateral position needs only the camera height and the horizon row, which is why e_lat survives a badly known lens.

Finding the horizon rowcamera.py · calibrate()

On a real car the horizon row depends on how the camera is mounted, so it has to be measured. One frame of a straight track is enough. The gap between the lines shrinks linearly with v, and it reaches zero exactly at the horizon.

Calibration in one line: fit gap = a·v + b on a straight track, then vh = −b/a. The slope also measures the track width (a = 2w/h).
