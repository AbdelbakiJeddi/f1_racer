How the F1 racer finds the track

Demo of the vision pipeline in f1_racer: one 128×128 camera frame in, three steering numbers out. Every figure runs the project's own detector code, ported to the browser and checked against the Python.

The pipeline, livesynthetic drive · labs/helpers/vision_preview.py

The car weaves while the track bends left and right. On every frame the detector finds both yellow lines on two horizontal bands, takes their midpoints, and turns them into errors.

The driving policy never sees pixels. It receives three numbers from this detector: e_lat (where the centre is), e_heading (where the track is going) and confidence (how sure the detector is).

Order of the data: mask → scan → centre → camera → errors. No neural network: each block is a few lines of arithmetic, cheap enough for thousands of simulated cars per step.

What the policy receivesf1_racer_rl/…/mdp/observations.py · vision_track_state

The actor network gets a 7-number observation every control step. Three come from the camera and four from the car's own sensors.

#valuesourcemeaning
0e_latcameratrack centre sideways, ±1 = on a boundary, + = right
1e_headingcameratrack direction vs car nose, rad, + = heads right
2confidencecamera0 = nothing seen … 1 = both lines clear
3–5vx, vy, ωzvehiclebody velocities, yaw rate
6steervehiclefront knuckle angle
The critic sees privileged ground truth during training. The actor sees only what a real car can compute. If the detector lies, the policy steers wrong, and no amount of PPO training fixes that.

Two implementationslab vs production

Lab detector · NumPy

labs/lanedet/, built block by block in labs/01–08*.ipynb. Readable, one frame at a time, finds every line and pairs them by expected width. Pages 1–5 follow this one.

GPU detector · PyTorch

src/leatherback_racer/vision/track_detector.py. Same five blocks on a batch of N cars at once, used during RL training. Simpler scan and centre steps. Page 6 opens it up.
